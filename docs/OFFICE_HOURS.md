# Office Hours — SeatFour

Status: autonomous pre-build diagnostic; hypotheses, not customer evidence.

## Specific customer

The paying ICP is the events manager or owner of a 1–3-location independent game store that runs recurring open-play nights and regularly has partially filled tables. The first player segment is an adult who wants in-person play but does not already have a reliable group.

## Painful status quo

Stores announce events through Discord, Facebook, calendars, and paper sign-up sheets. Players ask “is anyone playing Friday?”, organizers manually balance experience levels, and a single no-show can collapse a four-person game. The real competitor is that chat-plus-spreadsheet workaround, not another marketplace.

## Desperate use case

At 3 PM Friday, an events manager has three committed Catan players and one empty seat. Without a fourth person, the table may cancel, players lose trust, and the store loses dwell time and food/product sales. The buyer needs a confirmed, compatible fourth player—not generic community software.

## Narrowest paid wedge

One city, five partner stores, three recurring nights, and six games. Players submit game, pace, availability, and radius. SeatFour recommends an existing store-hosted table and runs a ten-minute hold plus confirmation reminder. Stores pay only after the concierge pilot demonstrates filled seats.

## Approaches considered

1. **Concierge roster (recommended, S / low risk):** store CSV + player form + human confirmations. Fastest way to test attendance lift.
2. **Rules-based matching (M / medium risk):** explicit table inventory and deterministic ranking, as modeled in this prototype. Transparent and cheap.
3. **Two-sided social network (XL / high risk):** profiles, chat, reputation, hosted games, and recommendations. Ideal only after density exists.

The prototype chooses approach 2 to make the core operation tangible, while the market test should start with approach 1.

## Observation and validation

Shadow two store managers during event setup; record every tool switch and table intervention. Recruit 30 players from existing channels. Run three weekly events without a guided product demo. Measure eligible requests → accepted holds → check-ins → completed games, cancellation recovery time, and manager minutes saved. Demand is five stores agreeing to pay at least $99/month after the pilot—not compliments or sign-ups.

## Future-fit

As hobbies become an antidote to remote-work isolation, local venues need reliable formation rather than more broadcast channels. SeatFour becomes more useful if it owns verified attendance and compatibility data. It becomes less useful if stores can fill every table organically or dominant community platforms ship venue-grade roster tooling.

## Assignment

Get one store to hand over the next three event rosters and let SeatFour manually fill five empty seats. Observe the manager using the workflow without coaching.
